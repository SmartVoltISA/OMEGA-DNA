# DNA structural levels

## Purpose

Build the biological object from observed physical structure before applying the OMEGA relational model.

## Level L0 — Atoms

Relevant atoms include C, H, N, O and P. At this level the primary objects are atoms and their chemical interactions.

**Architectural question:** which measurable interactions create stable molecular structure?

## Level L1 — Chemical bonds

Covalent bonds form the persistent molecular framework. Their geometry, energy and connectivity constrain the possible states of the molecule.

**Relation type:** chemical bond.

## Level L2 — Nucleotide

A nucleotide contains a nitrogenous base, a sugar and phosphate group. Nucleotides form the repeating units of DNA polymers.

**Architectural role:** reusable structural node composed of internal relations.

## Level L3 — Nucleotides in a strand

Nucleotides are connected through the sugar-phosphate backbone. Their order creates a directional polymer.

**Important distinction:** sequence order is not itself a chemical bond; it is an ordered structural relation generated by connectivity.

## Level L4 — Base pairing

In double-stranded DNA, complementary bases form specific hydrogen-bonding patterns: A pairs with T, and G pairs with C under canonical Watson-Crick pairing.

Base pairing should be represented as a constrained relation, not merely as a symbol substitution.

## Level L5 — Double helix

Two strands are coupled by complementary pairing and stabilize a higher-order geometry. The strands constrain one another while remaining individually structured.

**Architectural question:** when does a relation between two structures become a new higher-order structure?

## L6 — Sequence as memory

A sequence is a persistent molecular state that can be copied. The information is physically embodied in molecular structure; it is not independent of the molecule carrying it.

## L7 — Chromatin and accessibility

DNA is organized with proteins into chromatin. Higher-order organization changes physical accessibility and therefore constrains which molecular interactions can occur.

## L8 — Functional regions

Genes, regulatory regions and other DNA elements participate in larger interaction networks. Function depends on sequence, structure, molecular context and cellular state.

## L9 — Maintenance loops

Replication, repair and recombination are processes that copy, preserve or modify DNA structure. These processes create feedback between molecular state and maintenance machinery.

## L10 — Evolutionary persistence

Mutation changes molecular state; selection and other population processes influence which states persist across generations.

## Working chain

`atom → bond → nucleotide → backbone → pairing → coupled helix → chromatin → functional context → maintenance → inheritance`

This chain is a research representation, not a claim that biology has a single universal hierarchy.

## Critical distinction

Three concepts must remain separate throughout the project:

1. **Physical relation** — measurable molecular interaction.
2. **Structural relation** — constraint created by organization and geometry.
3. **Informational relation** — correspondence between molecular states and reproducible biological effects.

Collapsing these into one generic "connection" would make the model less testable.
