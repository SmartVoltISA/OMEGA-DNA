## Scope

This document compares the OMEGA-DNA architectural mapping with established molecular biology and expert reviews. It is a literature cross-check, not a validation of the OMEGA hypothesis.

## Key literature cross-checks

### 1. DNA as complementary physical structure

NCBI molecular biology references describe DNA as two complementary antiparallel nucleotide chains with a sugar-phosphate backbone, base pairing and a double-helical structure. Each strand can serve as a template for synthesis of a complementary strand.

References:
- NCBI Bookshelf, *The Structure and Function of DNA*, Molecular Biology of the Cell.
- NCBI Bookshelf, *Biochemistry, DNA Structure*.

### 2. Sequence is information, but physical organization matters

Established biology identifies nucleotide order as the carrier of hereditary information. At the same time, DNA exists as a three-dimensional molecule and is packaged into chromatin, which changes physical access to DNA-templated processes.

### 3. Chromatin is dynamic

Expert reviews describe chromatin accessibility as a combined outcome of DNA sequence, nucleosome organization/modification and ATP-dependent remodeling. This directly supports representing accessibility as a state variable and protein/chromatin interactions as typed relations rather than as static sequence symbols.

### 4. Replication and repair are maintenance processes

Reviews of chromatin replication and genome integrity describe mechanisms that reproduce chromatin organization during DNA replication and coordinate chromatin changes with DNA repair. This supports the OMEGA distinction between stored state and the machinery/process that maintains it.

### 5. Genome architecture is spatial and dynamic

Recent Nature Reviews work describes cohesin-mediated loop extrusion and sister-chromatid cohesion as mechanisms contributing to genome organization, regulation, repair and chromosome segregation. Recent reviews of DNA double-strand break repair also emphasize chromosome topology, chromatin loops, nuclear compartments and dynamic genome organization.

## Important conclusion from the comparison

The relational architecture is broadly compatible with established biology across scales. However, compatibility is not novelty and is not proof of predictive advantage.

The scientific question therefore becomes narrower and stronger:

**Can the typed relational representation predict something measurably better than established sequence/structure models under controlled conditions?**
